Neighborhoods / Marylebone

Moving to Marylebone

Village shops, garden squares, and the easiest central postcode to actually feel settled in.

Central London Bakerloo & Jubilee lines

What you'll actually be looking at

Georgian conversions

Classic townhouses split into flats, high ceilings, steps from the high street.

Mews houses

Small cobbled properties tucked behind the main streets, rare and expensive.

Mansion blocks

Red-brick Edwardian buildings around Chiltern Street and Baker Street, mostly with a doorman.

Who it suits

Professionals wanting a central address without Mayfair prices Empty-nesters downsizing centrally Harley Street–adjacent professionals Anyone who wants village life inside Zone 1

Getting around

  • Bakerloo and Jubilee lines at Baker Street, plus Marylebone mainline for Chiltern Railways and buses 18, 205, and 453.
  • About 10 minutes to Waterloo via the Bakerloo, 12 to Victoria via the Jubilee.

The honest take

Why people move here

A genuine village high street — independent shops, a Sunday farmers' market — inside Zone 1. That combination barely exists anywhere else in central London.

What catches people out

You're paying central-London prices for often quite compact, older properties. Modern space here is genuinely scarce.

Where it sits on price

Below Mayfair and Knightsbridge, above most of Zone 2 — one of the more expensive "village" postcodes precisely because it's this central and this walkable.
