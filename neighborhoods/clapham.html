Neighborhoods / Clapham

Moving to Clapham

Young professionals, commons, and a commute that actually works.

South West London Northern line Clapham Junction interchange

Clapham is the default answer when a friend asks where to live in South West London — for a reason. Here's what that actually means once you're the one moving in.

What you'll actually be looking at

Clapham's housing stock is dominated by one thing: Victorian and Edwardian terraces split into flats. Whole houses exist, but they're the exception, not the rule.

Converted terraces

Two- and three-bed splits of period houses. The largest share of the market — a conversion with a garden share is the default Clapham flat.

Mansion blocks

Purpose-built flats from the 1900s–30s, mostly around the Old Town and North Side. More storage and soundproofing, no garden of your own.

New-build

A handful of newer developments near Clapham Junction. Lifts and porters, less character — convenience over period charm.

Who it suits

Young professionals sharing Couples pre-kids Remote workers who want a green space nearby Families staying through the school search

Getting around

  • Northern line — Clapham North, Common, and South, all a short walk from each other.
  • Clapham Junction — Overground and National Rail at one of the busiest interchanges in Europe, plus buses 35, 37, 88, and 137. A real commute asset, and a real Friday-night crowd.
  • Into the centre — about 7 minutes to Victoria by National Rail from Clapham Junction, 12 direct to Waterloo on the Northern line.

The honest take

Why people move here

The Common. Not a park — 220 acres of it, with lidos, markets, and a skyline you can actually see. It's the reason people justify the rent.

What catches people out

Clapham High Street on a Saturday night is not the same neighbourhood as Clapham on a Tuesday morning. And period conversions rarely come with outdoor space of their own, Common excluded.

Where it sits on price

Clapham prices above Brixton and Tooting, and below Battersea's riverside developments or Chelsea. Within Zone 2 South West London, it's mid-to-upper — you're paying a premium for the Common and the transport, not for space.
