Neighborhoods / Greenwich

Moving to Greenwich

Maritime history, a park with a view of the whole city, and prices that still make sense — south bank, just past the river's tightest bend.

South East London DLR

What you'll actually be looking at

Georgian terraces

The historic core around Greenwich Park and the market, among the best-preserved period stock in London.

Victorian conversions

Further from the centre, more affordable, similar character to other South East London areas.

Peninsula new-build

Modern towers and low-rise near the O2, built over the last two decades.

Who it suits

Buyers wanting genuine history and a park on the doorstep Families priced out of similar stock in South West London People happy to trade central speed for space and character

Getting around

  • DLR at Cutty Sark, Southeastern and Thameslink rail from Greenwich station, plus buses 129, 177, 188, and 199 and — distinctively — the Thames Clippers river bus.
  • About 10–12 minutes by rail to London Bridge or Waterloo East, 25 to Victoria.

The honest take

Why people move here

Greenwich Park, the market, and the Georgian core give it a small-town feel that's genuinely hard to find this close to central London.

What catches people out

It sits slightly apart from the rest of London's transport grid — journeys that aren't toward the centre can take real planning.

Where it sits on price

Below the equivalent Georgian stock in Islington or Chelsea, and below Canary Wharf's new-build prices just across the water — one of the better value historic postcodes in London.
