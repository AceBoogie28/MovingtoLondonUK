Neighborhoods / Chelsea

Moving to Chelsea

The most established expat postcode in the city, and priced like it — north bank, facing Battersea across the water.

West London District & Circle lines

What you'll actually be looking at

Garden square terraces

The classic Chelsea address, houses and grand flats facing a private communal garden.

Riverside mansion blocks

Edwardian buildings along the Embankment, some with river views.

Mews & cottages

Former stable buildings off the King's Road, small and among the most expensive per square foot in London.

Who it suits

Established professionals and families International buyers wanting a recognised prime address People prioritising quiet and settled over nightlife

Getting around

  • District and Circle lines via Sloane Square or South Kensington, backed by a high-frequency bus corridor along the King's Road (11, 19, 22, 170) that does real work given how thin the tube coverage otherwise is.
  • Sloane Square to Victoria is about 2 minutes; Waterloo is 15–20 depending on the connection.

The honest take

Why people move here

The garden squares and the King's Road together give Chelsea a village calm that's rare this close to the centre.

What catches people out

Tube coverage is genuinely thin. Check the actual walk to a station before assuming "Chelsea" means well-connected.

Where it sits on price

One of the most expensive postcodes in London outright — above Notting Hill and South Kensington, competing directly with Knightsbridge and Belgravia.
