Registering with the NHS

What newcomers need to know about GP registration and private cover.

Registering with a GP

Registration is more open than most newcomers expect: you don't need ID, proof of address, or proof of immigration status to register with a GP practice in England, and you can use a temporary address if you don't have a permanent one yet — there's no reason to wait until you've settled.

The fastest route is online, through the NHS App or the NHS website's Find a GP tool — over 6,150 practices now support registering this way, with no need to visit in person or fill in a paper form. The form itself takes about 15 minutes; most practices then confirm registration within about 5 working days.

What the NHS actually covers

Care through the NHS is free at the point of use for most services once you're registered, but most visa holders already paid for this as part of their application — the Immigration Health Surcharge (IHS) is a separate, mandatory fee added to most visa applications, currently £1,035 per year for most adults and £776 per year for students, their dependants, and Youth Mobility Scheme applicants. It's paid upfront for the full length of your visa, not per use. Some services still carry their own charges even once you're registered — prescriptions and dental care being the two most common.

When you need care before you're registered

  • 111 — the NHS's non-emergency phone and online service, for anything that needs medical advice but isn't a 999 emergency.
  • Walk-in and urgent treatment centres — accessible without a GP registration, for things that need same-day attention but aren't emergencies.
  • A&E — for genuine emergencies, and available regardless of registration status.

Private healthcare

Many relocators, especially on an employer relocation package, add private health insurance for faster specialist access — as a supplement to NHS registration, not a replacement for it. You'll still want to register with a GP either way.

Confirm before you rely on this. IHS rates and NHS registration processes are set by the Home Office and NHS England respectively, and both are revised periodically — confirm current figures before budgeting around them.
