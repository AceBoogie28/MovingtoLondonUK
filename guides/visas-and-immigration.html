Which UK Visa Is Actually Right for You?

A plain-English breakdown of the most common routes people use to relocate to London — what each one requires, and who it tends to suit. This is an orientation, not legal advice.

Every route below has real eligibility rules, real costs, and rules that change more often than almost anything else on this site — nothing here replaces checking the current requirements yourself or with a registered adviser. What this guide can do is tell you which routes actually apply to different situations, so you know what to go and verify.

Start with your reason for moving

The right visa route depends heavily on why you're relocating — a confirmed job offer, a partner or spouse already in the UK, remote work for a non-UK employer, or an intent to start a business will each point toward a different route.

The routes relocators actually use

  • Skilled Worker — the most common route for people moving for a specific job. Requires a job offer from a Home Office–licensed sponsor, in a role at RQF Level 6 or above (degree-level), meeting the current salary threshold — both worth confirming directly, since they're revised periodically.
  • Global Talent — for people already recognised as leaders or emerging leaders in academia, research, arts, or tech. No job offer required, but it does require endorsement from a relevant named body, and can count toward settlement in three or five years depending on the endorsement.
  • Family / partner — for people joining a spouse, partner, or family member who's a British citizen or settled in the UK. Its own income and relationship-evidence requirements, separate from the work-based routes.
  • Ancestry — available to some Commonwealth citizens with a UK-born grandparent. One of the more flexible routes here, but genuinely dependent on your specific family history.
  • Student, then switch — a common path: study in the UK first, then move onto a work route — often the Graduate visa, then Skilled Worker — once you're established.
  • Innovator Founder — for people starting an innovative, viable, scalable business in the UK. Requires endorsement from an approved body — a narrower audience, with requirements specific enough that this isn't a route to self-assess.

This is an orientation, not legal advice. Visa rules are set by the Home Office and change on their own schedule — always confirm current eligibility, costs, and processing times directly with GOV.UK or a solicitor regulated by the OISC before making a decision based on this page.

Timeline: what to expect

Most routes involve an online application, biometric enrolment, and a decision window that can range from a few weeks to a few months depending on the route and time of year. Once your visa route is clear, the rest of the relocation timeline — banking, housing, school enrolment — tends to fall into place around it. That's covered in the other guides in this series.
