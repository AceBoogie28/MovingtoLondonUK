Setting Up a UK Bank Account Remotely

What you can arrange before you arrive, and what has to wait until you land.

UK banks are built around one assumption that doesn't hold for relocators: that you already have a UK address. That's the sticking point behind almost everything below — some providers have found ways around it, most haven't.

Before you arrive

  • Digital-first accounts — Monzo, Starling, Revolut, Wise, and Monese are built to be opened before arrival and give you a real UK sort code and account number, without needing a UK address first. Eligibility and exact requirements vary by provider and change often, so check current terms directly.
  • Multi-currency accounts — Wise and Revolut both offer multi-currency accounts you can open from your home country, useful as a bridge for receiving pay or paying deposits before a full UK account exists.
  • Some high-street banks let you start early — Barclays, for example, lets you begin an application online up to 90 days before your move, but you'll still need to complete it at a branch once you've landed.
  • Specialist "international" banking — HSBC Expat and Barclays International are structured specifically for internationally mobile people, usually based offshore in Jersey or the Isle of Man, with multi-currency accounts built for exactly this transition.

Once you're here

A full high-street current account still expects proof of UK address — a signed tenancy agreement, a utility bill, or a letter from your employer or council usually works. Your NI number and passport or eVisa are typically requested regardless of which bank you choose. And UK credit history starts from zero — it doesn't transfer from abroad, which is worth knowing before you need a mortgage or a credit card down the line.

Confirm before you rely on this. Which accounts are open to non-residents, and on what terms, changes often — check current eligibility directly with the provider before making a decision based on this page.
